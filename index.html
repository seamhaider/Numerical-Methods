Theory & Background

Numerical Integration

Numerical integration is used when an integral cannot be solved analytically, or when the function is only known at discrete points. The Newton–Cotes formulas are a family of methods that approximate integrals by fitting polynomials through equally spaced points.

  • Trapezoidal Rule → fits a straight line (degree 1 polynomial)
  • Simpson’s 1/3 Rule → fits a quadratic (degree 2 polynomial)
  • Simpson’s 3/8 Rule → fits a cubic (degree 3 polynomial)
  • Weddle’s Rule → fits a 6th-degree polynomial

The higher the degree of the polynomial, the more accurate the approximation, but also more complex.

🔹 Simpson’s 3/8 Rule (Cubic Approximation)

Divide the interval [a, b] into 3 equal subintervals → 4 points: x₀, x₁, x₂, x₃. A cubic polynomial is fitted through these points. 

∫ₐᵇ f(x) dx ≈ (3h/8)[f(x₀) + 3f(x₁) + 3f(x₂) + f(x₃)]
h = (b - a)/3

Composite Rule: Apply repeatedly for larger intervals.

Error Term:

E = -(3h⁵/80) f⁽⁴⁾(ξ),  ξ ∈ [a, b]

Strength: More accurate than trapezoidal.

Limitation: Subinterval must be multiple of 3.

🔹 Weddle’s Rule (6th-Degree Approximation)

Divide the interval [a, b] into 6 equal subintervals → 7 points. A 6th-degree polynomial is fitted. 

∫ₐᵇ f(x) dx ≈ (3h/10)[
f(x₀) + 5f(x₁) + f(x₂) + 6f(x₃) + 
f(x₄) + 5f(x₅) + f(x₆)
]
h = (b - a)/6

Error Term:

E = -(h⁷/140) f⁽⁷⁾(ξ),  ξ ∈ [a, b]

Strength: Very high accuracy.

Limitation: More complex, requires multiple of 6.
